[RUNNING HEAD] DEVELOPMENT OF THE COLONIAL HABIT 471

Palaeozoic graptolites, and their coenoecia often show a close resemblance to those of graptolites.

Rhabdopleura forms intricately-branched colonies consisting of creeping stolons, irregularly dichotomously branched, from which the tubular thecae of separate autozooids rise above the substrate (Fig. 212, B). At the ends of the stolons there are immature terminal zooids. The stolons are covered with thin chitinoid cuticle and enclosed in cylindrical tubes produced by a secretion of the terminal zooids. Later the zooid perforates the external tube with a round aperture and builds its own cylindrical theca above its margins. From time to time one of the buds formed, instead of developing into an autozooid, halts its development and becomes transformed into a terminal zooid, and its stolon begins to grow longer. The stolon thus becomes bifurcated. Sooner or later the terminal zooid may develop into an autozooid, and in that case growth of the stolon in that direction ceases. Rhabdopleura autozooids are of three kinds—male, female, and asexual individuals (A. Schepotieff, 1906, 1907). In addition, some of the buds formed along the stolon do not develop, become filled with reserve matter, and form the so-called hibernating zooids (hibernacula). In Rhabdopleura there is the same kind of alternation of zooids as in Bryozoa and hydroids.
