Fig. 214. Forms of ascidian colonies.

A—part of section through one of the cormidia of a Botryllus colony. B—young (18 days old) colony of Diplosoma gelatinosa: o2-o5—zooids of first generation, budded directly on oozooid; b2-b7—zooids of second generation, budded on blastozooids. C—beginning of formation of a Clavellina colony, oozooid with stolon and two buds. D—shoot of colony of Perophora hutchisoni with buds of various ages (from Korschelt and Heider). E—Polyclinium constellatum, surface view of part of colony: the numerous zooids are united by their cloacal canals to the common cormidial cloacae (from Huus). 1—cloacae of separate zooids; 2—their outlets into common cloaca of cormidium; 3—outlet of common cloaca; 4—cerebral ganglion; 5—cerebral gland; 6—oral siphon; 7—branchial sac; 8—endostyle; 9—tunic; 10—its vessels; 11—young buds; 12—gut; 13—stomach; 14—buds (blastozooids); 15—root processes of stolon; 16—epicardial tubes, i.e. processes of the mid-gut of the oozooid, from which the endodermal organs of the blastozooids are formed; 17—heart.
