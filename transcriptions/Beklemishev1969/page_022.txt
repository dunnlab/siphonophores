Thus all three processes—formation of an evagination, of a septum, and of a polypide—are closely linked together and lead to formation of separate, individualised zooids. In Stenolaemata the growth process outstrips the other processes, and therefore a growth zone develops with constant growth at one end while the processes of formation of septa and of separate zooids take place at the other end.

This method of budding is certainly a long step forward in the development of colonial individuality. There is some analogy among coelenterates in, for instance, colonies of the thecate hydroids (Sertulariidae, etc.), in which a terminal growth zone is found not only on the hydrorhiza stolon but also on the vertical stem (hydrocaulon).

In most Stenolaemata the cavities of mature zooids are entirely separate and communicate with each other only by pores. In two families, however (Horneridae and Lichenoporidae), further integration of the colony has taken place. When new zooids are formed in these families the septum that separates the coelom of a new individual from the common cavity of the growth zone does not reach the terminal membrane and is not attached to it. The coeloms of all the individuals therefore remain in communication with the common colonial or hypostegal coelom (Fig. 211, D). A peculiar organisation develops: the basal wall of the colony (in the incrustational Lichenopora) and all the interzooidal walls remain single, as in other Stenolaemata, while the free walls of the cystids are double.

The double wall consists of the following layers, starting at the outside: (i) cuticle; (ia) outer calcareous layer (only in Hornera violacea); (ii) ectoderm of the outer wall, or gymnocyst; (iii) mesoderm of the gymnocyst; (iv) hypostegal (common colonial) coelom; (v) mesoderm of the inner wall, or of the cryptocyst (outer layer); (vi) ectoderm of the cryptocyst (outer layer); (vii) calcareous layer of the cryptocyst; (viii) ectoderm of the cryptocyst (inner layer); (ix) mesoderm of the cryptocyst (inner layer); then comes the coelom of the zooid.

The gymnocyst is the common covering of the colony; at the edges of the aperture it becomes entirely transformed into the terminal membrane, while the outer layers of the cryptocyst bend round the edges of the calcareous tube and pass into the inner lining of the latter. Around the aperture the hypostegal cavity communicates with the coelom of the zooid. The cryptocyst forms the wall of the cystid, partly shutting off its cavity from the cavities of adjacent cystids and from the hypostegal cavity.
