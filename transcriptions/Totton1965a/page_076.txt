Presence in the deep-scattering layer

There is, according to E. G. Barham (1963), a striking relation between the depths of recorded deep-scattering layers of the ocean waters and concentration of siphonophores. The abstract of his interesting paper reads: 'Bathyscaphe divers in the San Diego Trough have revealed a close spatial relation between siphonophores and the deep-scattering layer as recorded by precision depth-recording echo-sounders. Measurements of gas-bubbles* within the floatation structures of Nanomia bijuga captured in a closing-net in an ascended scattering layer indicated that these are very close to the resonant size for 12/kcy/sec. sound. Because such organisms are capable of making prolonged vertical migrations, and are widespread geographically, they are very probably the major causes of stratified zones of scattering throughout the oceans of the world.'

Specific identifications need confirmation.

[FOOTNOTE] * Dr. Barham (personal communication) and colleagues found concentrations of CO up to 90% on analysis of the gas.
