Male and female gonophores are borne by different eudoxids. The second gonophore to be budded is the mirror-image of the first. The whole eudoxid and the male gonophore were described and figured in detail by Totton (1932, text-figs. 25, 26) as Eudoxia russelli. Although Leuckart's (1853) description and figure of his Eudoxia campanella differ in some respects (see Totton, 1932, p. 356), I now consider them to be synonymous. It was a common eudoxid found by Leuckart at Nice. Eudoxia messanensis of Gegenbaur (1854) is not synonymous; it is probably the eudoxid of Lensia conoidea. I discussed the literature of this in 1954 (pp. 128–29).

Progression:   Notes on my observations will be found in Totton (1954, p. 129).
