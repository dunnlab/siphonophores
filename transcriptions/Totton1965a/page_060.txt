FIG. 19.   Agalma elegans (Sars). 'Athorybia' larvae
A, an early larva, × 62, showing bracts (coloured blue), bracteal canals (red), and the first of a ring of palpons; B, an early stage in metamorphosis, × 16. That side of the upper end which carries the pneumatophore has elongated to carry the latter above the ring of coronal bracts. Two nectophores have developed and are functional. The protozooid has taken up a latero-terminal position leaving the second gastrozooid at the end

Pn = pneumatophore; N = nectophore; T.larv = larval tentacle; Gz2, 3 = secondary gastrozooids; Nec = nectostyle; Bg = basigaster; Pal = palpon; Pr.Z = protozooid (from Totton, 1956, figs. 2, 6).

FIG. 20.   Agalma elegans (Sars)
A juvenile specimen × 11. Nectosome and siphosome have elongated in opposite directions. The nectostyle can still be seen opposite to the pedicel of the first nectophore. Note the small size of the protozooid. Definitive (secondary) stem bracts coloured green. Tentacles not shown except for part of the larval one of the protozooid (from Totton, 1956, fig. 9).
