Eudoxid phase

(See Vogt, 1854, pp. 114–16.) Male and female gonophores are borne on separate 'colonies', according to Vogt (p. 112). He figured the saccus of the tentillum, and examined twenty-five specimens. The gonophores, which are in fact the adults, break loose from the attached cormidia and swim freely for several days.

Colour (see Vogt, 1854, p. 115):   Male manubrium vermilion, female colourless.

Sulculeolaria biloba (Sars, 1846)
Text-figures 83–86

Galeolaria australis Bigelow, 1911b, plate 5, figure 8; Bigelow, 1918

Sars gave accurate figures; the last review was by Totton (1954) where further accurate figures are to be found. There has been much confusion between this species and forms called 'australis' by certain authors, making futile a study of much of the literature. For example, Bigelow & Sears' (1937) Galetta australis is Sulculeolaria turgida. I have re-examined the material.
